Problem P15.48 · Electric force

Four charges on a square

Point charges +Q+Q+Q and −Q-Q−Q sit on alternate corners of a square of side ddd.

  1. (a)Find the net force, magnitude and direction, on each charge.
  2. (b)A fifth charge +Q+Q+Q is placed at the centre of the square. What net force acts on it?

Letters A to D name the corners for this solution. Use Next or the → key to step through, and drag the drawing to tilt it.

Check. Tip-to-tail, the four forces close into a loop, so they add to zero. That is Newton's third law at work: forces inside an isolated system cancel in pairs.

Part (b) · Fifth charge

Add +Q+Q+Q at the centre

Call the new charge P. The centre is half a diagonal from every corner.

r=d22=d2⇒r2=d22r = \frac{d\sqrt2}{2} = \frac{d}{\sqrt2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r^2 = \frac{d^2}{2}r=2d2​​=2​d​⇒r2=2d2​

Part (b) · Pair the corners

Opposite corners cancel

Arrows in part (b) are drawn at half scale.

  1. BD both −Q-Q−Q: each pulls P toward itself with 2F02F_02F0​ equal and opposite, so they cancel
  2. AC both +Q+Q+Q: each pushes P away with 2F02F_02F0​ equal and opposite, so they cancel

Tip-to-tail, the four forces close into a loop.

Answer (b)

F⃗net=0\vec F_{\text{net}} = 0Fnet​=0

on the charge at the centre.

Every force on P is matched by an equal and opposite force from the opposite corner. Put another way, the four corner charges make zero electric field at the centre, so F⃗=QE⃗=0\vec F = Q\vec E = 0F=QE=0.

Going further

Zero force, but not stable

Height now shows the potential energy of P. Force points downhill, and at the centre the surface is level, which is why F=0F = 0F=0 there.

Nudge P toward A or C (+Q+Q+Q): the surface rises that way, so P is pushed back and rocks about the centre.

Nudge P toward B or D (−Q-Q−Q): the surface falls away and P runs off. The centre is a saddle point, an unstable equilibrium. No arrangement of fixed charges can hold a charge in stable equilibrium (Earnshaw's theorem).
